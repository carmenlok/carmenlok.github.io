About

I work on inference serving for open-weight models, mostly in vLLM, vLLM-Omni and FlashInfer (CUDA graphs, 8-bit floating-point kernels on Blackwell workstation GPUs, full-duplex speech), and on RL training for diffusion models.

Benchmark work

My measurements of other people's pull requests, posted as comments on them. Each line links to my comment.

  • 2026-11-18Prefill CUDA graphs on SM120, measured (vLLM, my results on PR #64825) · on an RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B in 8-bit floating point, capturing prefill-sized graphs is about neutral with a fast host CPU, but cuts median time to first token by 30% with the host CPU capped at 3.0 GHz and by 64% at 1.2 GHz; decode speed does not change. A GPU trace follow-up showed the small extra cost at stock clock is host overhead, not padding
  • 2026-11-18Small-batch 8-bit floating-point matrix multiply on SM121, benchmarked (FlashInfer, my results on PR #9464, merged) · on a DGX Spark, the new small-batch tiles run 2.6× faster at batch sizes up to 32 on the narrow 256-column shape and 1.1 to 1.2× faster on wider shapes, with output bit-identical to main at all 168 points tested
  • 2026-11-188-bit floating-point batched matrix multiply on SM121, benchmarked (FlashInfer, my results on PR #8137) · on a DGX Spark, the new kernel runs the reported shape at batch size 1 in 126 µs instead of 486 µs (3.85×, and 1.7× faster than cuBLAS) and stays about 3× faster up to batch size 512; I also found it slower at batch size 2,048 on large shapes, down to 0.62× on the largest
  • 2026-10-01Humming vs Marlin kernels on SM121 (vLLM, quantization special interest group, my results on PR #56997) · validated the fix for the MXFP8 crash on SM121, with GSM8K accuracy matching Marlin, and gave the group the speed data for its kernel-order call: on Qwen3-30B-A3B at 1 to 128 concurrent requests, Humming runs level with Marlin (MXFP8 −8.5% to +1.4%, NVFP4 −3.8% to +3.1% output tokens per second)

In progress

Research I'm working on now. Nothing is published yet, so these have no links.

  • nowHeteroFlash · serving large mixture-of-experts models on mixed home hardware, a DGX Spark and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Max-Q cards, by streaming expert weights in from system memory and over the network. Starts from the idea in Hecate. So far, weight streaming is what limits decode speed, so I'm measuring how long each decode step waits for its experts to arrive, and testing two fixes: keeping often-used experts on the GPU, and fetching the next layer's experts early
  • nowExpert correlation for expert load balancing · studying which experts tend to be picked together for the same tokens, to see whether vLLM's expert parallel load balancer could use that when it places experts on GPUs
